## Larger Finding

The work the seller can do is real.

But standalone research tasks show a price ceiling.

Market pattern:

- Buyers will pay for information and filtering.
- But high prices usually come from responsibility, execution, resources, operational ownership, or result risk.
- As AI reduces the cost of pure information labor, pure research deliverables are more vulnerable to price compression.

## Implication

The next research question should not be:

Which other research task family exists?

The better question is:

Which small step beyond information increases price meaningfully while preserving AI leverage and avoiding heavy execution?

## New Research Direction

Value Ladder:

Information -> Verification -> Light Execution -> Transaction Evidence

Goal:

Find the smallest additional responsibility that moves a task from ~$100 into the $300-$1,000 range without turning it into:

- full operations
- local agent work
- sales execution
- procurement management
- compliance ownership
- project management-heavy execution

## Example

Remote Supplier Discovery alone:

- 3-5 supplier names and contacts
- product/spec fit
- basic recommendation

Likely price:

- $100-$150 fixed

Remote Supplier Discovery + RFQ Evidence:

- supplier shortlist
- real supplier replies
- quote / MOQ / lead time
- certification proof or capability evidence
- comparison table
- recommendation

Potential:

- may justify higher pricing while remaining mostly remote and AI-leveraged.

## Next Step

Stop horizontal task-family testing.

Start testing value-ladder variants:

- What extra proof/verification/execution step do buyers pay more for?
- Where does price jump without unacceptable execution burden?
