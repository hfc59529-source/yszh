Status: STRUCTURE LEARNINGS — FIRST THREE TASK FAMILIES

The first three task-family tests produced useful market-structure evidence.

## 1. Public-Source Comparative Research + Recommendation

Decision:

- WATCH / Secondary Candidate

Observed structure:

- Value per task can be decent.
- The task exists as an independent buyer need.
- But strict-fit market density is weak.

Interpretation:

- Useful as a secondary offer or add-on.
- Not strong enough as the first primary cash-flow SKU.

## 2. Lead Research / Qualified Lead List

Decision:

- KILL AS PRIMARY SKU — REFRAME AS INPUT LAYER

Observed structure:

- Market density is high.
- Standalone list/qualification work is low-priced.
- Higher-value work tends to move downstream into outreach, SDR, campaign ops, automation, meetings, or closing.

Interpretation:

- Lead research is useful as an input layer.
- It is weak as a standalone primary SKU.

## 3. Account Intelligence

Decision:

- WEAK STANDALONE MARKET — EMBEDDED CAPABILITY

Observed structure:

- Capability is valuable.
- Buyers rarely describe it as a standalone Upwork task.
- It appears inside GTM, RevOps, SDR/BDR, ABM, campaign, automation, or data-platform workflows.

Interpretation:

- Not a strong independent purchasing unit.
- Keep as an embedded capability.

## Larger Pattern

The evidence suggests:

- The closer the task is to pure research, the easier it is to buy independently, but the density may be weak.
- The closer the task is to revenue, the higher the value, but buyers often purchase the full execution loop instead of the intermediate judgment layer.
